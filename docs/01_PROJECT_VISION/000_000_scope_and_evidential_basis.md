## Source Extract
This report treats the Backrooms primarily as an *affective design problem*, not a lore compendium. The focus is the interaction between liminality, uncanny architecture, nostalgia, environmental psychology, spatial disorientation, urban-exploration aesthetics, isolation, derealisation-like unreality, and curiosity-driven exploration. The evidence base combines the original 4chan post and image history, the major fan canons on Wikidot and Fandom, community discussions that explicitly reject “monster-first” interpretations, academic work on uncanny places, wayfinding, nostalgia, loneliness and solitude, environmental storytelling, curiosity in games, and research on abandoned or ruinous spaces. Two contextual facts matter. First, the Backrooms began as a very short creepypasta attached to a single “wrong-feeling” room photograph on 4chan in May 2019, using vivid sensory language—moist carpet, “mono-yellow”, fluorescent hum-buzz, randomly segmented empty rooms, and a scale estimate absurd enough to feel cosmically incorrect. Second, the actual photograph’s source was traced in 2024 to a 2002 renovation image from a HobbyTown-linked property in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, which matters because the image is not surrealist fabrication: it is reality, slightly off, which is exactly why it bites. citeturn22view0turn24search0turn5search1turn5search4turn5search10

What follows therefore asks a stricter question than “what is the Backrooms?” It asks: **what structural conditions make a space feel like the Backrooms, and how can those conditions be reproduced in a game without collapsing into gimmick, lore bloat, or chase-horror cliché?** Community discourse repeatedly points in the same direction: many fans find the concept strongest when the fear comes from scale, absence, monotony, isolation, and incomprehensible architecture rather than from an overgrown bestiary. ## Design Rules
- Preserve the Backrooms as an affective, architectural, liminal experience rather than a monster-first horror game.
- Every feature must serve scale, absence, isolation, mystery, uncertainty, exploration, or spatial wrongness.
- Do not solve unease with constant threat, lore dumping, entities, or objective spam.

## Implementation Notes
- Prefer data-driven definitions, stable schemas, and testable systems.
- Any future code must be traceable back to a design rule or emotional target.
- If a mechanic makes the player feel powerful, safe, or fully certain, justify it as temporary and suspicious.

## Codex Instructions
- Read related files before implementation.
- Do not invent new mechanics that contradict the emotional target.
- When uncertain, preserve ambiguity and ask for a design decision rather than filling with generic horror.
